Abstract

The Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) is a scalar-tensor theory in which proper time is a dynamical field $\phi$ that couples universally to all matter via a conformal metric $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = A^2(\phi) g_{\mu\nu}$. In deep gravitational potential wells, high ambient density suppresses the local gradient of this field — a mechanism called Temporal Shear screening — with the degree of suppression scaling with a body's gravitational compactness $\Phi/c^2$. Because Earth and Moon have different compactness, differential screening predicts a violation of the Strong Equivalence Principle manifest as a synodic Earth-Moon range modulation $\delta r = 13\eta\cos D$, where $D$ is the Moon-Sun elongation angle and $\eta$ is the Nordtvedt parameter. The expected amplitude is at the millimetre level for $\eta \sim 10^{-4}$.

This work analyses 26,207 raw Lunar Laser Ranging O–C residuals from the public INPOP19a ephemeris archives (Paris Observatory, Geoazur), comprising $N = 25{,}445$ measurements from five international stations (1984–2019) after standard $6\sigma$ MAD outlier cleaning. The primary estimand is a precision-weighted synodic regression on the post-fit residual channel, with inverse station-variance weighting and a full systematic model that includes annual, monthly, and station-specific regressors.

The precision-weighted estimate is $\eta = -3.91 \times 10^{-4} \pm 5.63 \times 10^{-5}$ ($6.94\sigma$; $6.78\sigma$ cluster-robust). Sensitivity bounds bracket this estimand: unweighted full-systematic OLS gives $\eta = -4.06 \times 10^{-4} \pm 6.58 \times 10^{-5}$ ($6.17\sigma$; $6.52\sigma$ cluster-robust), while Cook's-Distance excision of 1,608 high-leverage points returns $\eta = -3.87 \times 10^{-4} \pm 4.95 \times 10^{-5}$ ($7.82\sigma$; $8.65\sigma$ cluster-robust), confirming the detection is not driven by outliers. Because only five stations contribute, a wild cluster bootstrap with Webb 6-point weights — specifically designed for small-$G$ inference — gives a co-primary $95\%$ interval $[-5.37, -2.73] \times 10^{-4}$ ($5.56\sigma$ on the unweighted full-systematic model), confirming that the detection is not an artifact of unreliable small-cluster asymptotics. The signal strengthens monotonically as controls are added, from $5.25\sigma$ ($\cos D$ only) to $6.94\sigma$ (precision-weighted), indicating that the extracted synodic modulation is not an artifact of omitted systematics. Robustness checks include a common-$\eta$ mixed model with station-specific systematics ($F(4, 25{,}410) = 1.19$, $p = 0.31$), leave-one-station-out meta-analysis ($I^2 = 0.0\%$), and a parametric bootstrap under the GR null (zero false positives in 10,000 trials; 95\% upper bound on rate $\approx 3 \times 10^{-4}$). Independent confirmation comes from a phase-locked new/full-moon differential ($\eta = -5.95 \times 10^{-4}$, $5.91\sigma$) and a frequency null scan with no significant non-synodic power. Cross-ephemeris validation on DE430 and orthogonality tests support residual-channel survival of the synodic component.

Source-level numerical refits of the INPOP or DE430 integrators with $\eta$ left free remain the critical open closure test. Ephemeris-absorption stress tests bound the residual-channel survival amplitude but do not replace a full dynamical integrator-level confirmation.

Code Availability: All data and analysis code required to reproduce the results presented in this work, including the full LLR residual processing pipeline, are available in the public repository.
