Abstract

The Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) proposes that the local rate of proper time is governed by a dynamical scalar field $\phi=\bar\phi+M_{\rm Pl}u$ rather than treated as a fixed geometric parameter. This field couples universally to all matter via the canonical unified metric $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = -A_{\rm loc}^2(u_{\rm grav}(\mathbf x))U_\mu U_\nu+A_{\rm sp}^2(u_{\rm drift}(t,\mathbf x))\Pi_{\mu\nu}+B(\phi)\Pi_\mu^\alpha\Pi_\nu^\beta\nabla_\alpha\phi\nabla_\beta\phi$ ($U_\mu\equiv-\nabla_\mu\bar\phi/\sqrt{2\bar X}$, $A_{\rm loc}(u_{\rm grav})=e^{\beta_A u_{\rm grav}}$, $A_{\rm sp}(u_{\rm drift})=e^{\beta_A u_{\rm drift}}=e^{-u_{\rm drift}}$; Paper 0, §2.2, Step 80). Temporal Shear is pinned where the ambient time-gradient is steep — weak-field language: $g \gg g_t \approx cH_0/2$ — by the environmental-state operator $\mathcal S_\Sigma(\mathcal E)$. The canonical pairwise benchmark gives an Earth–Moon response of order $10^{-14}$ and does not account for the reported range residual as a differential-fall signal; the analysis instead targets the synodic term in the post-fit residual channel, $\delta r = 13\eta_{\rm resid}\cos D$, where $D$ is the Moon-Sun elongation angle and $\eta_{\rm resid}$ is the residual-channel amplitude (an Observable Response Coefficient, distinct from the standard metric Nordtvedt parameter $\eta$). Evaluating both the Cassini Shapiro delay and the Earth–Moon laser path on the single solved two-branch solar profile $(k/\sqrt{2})q y^2+q^2 y^3=1$ ($k=16.03$, $g_t=cH_0/2$; Step 083) gives $y_{\rm tb}(1.6\,R_\odot)=2.16\times10^{-8}$ ($\gamma_{\rm PPN}-1=-4.32\times10^{-8}$, passing Cassini) and $y_{\rm tb}(1\text{ AU})=1.47\times10^{-5}$, which suppresses the direct scalar optical excursion across the Earth–Moon baseline to $\delta u_\odot^{\rm tb}=7.43\times10^{-16}\cos D$ ($\delta r_{\rm path}^{\rm tb}=-1.43\times10^{-4}$ mm, $\eta_{\rm tb}=-1.10\times10^{-8}$), $3.4\times10^4$ times below the measured post-fit INPOP19a amplitude $\eta_{\rm resid} = -(3.91\pm0.56)\times10^{-4}$ ($-5.08\pm0.73$ mm) and consistent with direct-fit LLR bounds. Because at a fixed cosmic epoch inside the screened Solar System ($A_{\rm sp}(t_0)=1$) TEP's spatial 3-metric $\tilde\gamma_{ij}=\gamma_{ij}=(1-2\Phi_N/c^2)\delta_{ij}$ is identical to GR's, the unscreened solar Newtonian potential differential $\Delta\Phi_{N,\odot}/c^2=2.54\times10^{-11}\cos D$ (which defines an unscreened reference ceiling $-\tfrac12 L\,\delta\phi_{\rm eff}\cos D=-4.85$ mm $\cos D$, $\eta_{\rm ceiling}=-3.73\times10^{-4}$ at $T_{\rm Moon}=0.9969$; Paper 0 Step 76, Step 083) cancels in the BCRS$\to$GCRS reduction identically to GR and would give $\gamma_{\rm PPN}-1=-2/3$ at Cassini if attributed to an unscreened solar scalar field; the measured $\eta_{\rm resid}$ is therefore reported as an empirical post-fit residual-channel detection whose dynamical or ephemeris-reduction transfer map awaits a source-level integrator refit.

This work analyses 26,207 raw Lunar Laser Ranging O–C residuals from the public INPOP19a ephemeris archives (Paris Observatory, Geoazur), comprising $N = 25{,}445$ measurements from five international stations (1984–2019) after standard $6\sigma$ MAD outlier cleaning. The primary estimand is a precision-weighted synodic regression on the post-fit residual channel, with inverse station-variance weighting and a full systematic model with annual, monthly, and thermal $\cos(2D)$ regressors and a common intercept.

The primary precision-weighted residual-channel estimate is $\eta_{\rm resid} = -3.91 \times 10^{-4} \pm 5.63 \times 10^{-5}$ (nominal WLS $6.94\sigma$; $6.78\sigma$ cluster-robust; wild cluster bootstrap 95% CI $[-5.37, -2.73]\times 10^{-4}$ across the $G=5$ station clusters). The amplitude stabilises in the band $-3.2$ to $-4.1 \times 10^{-4}$ across the estimator hierarchy, from cosD-only ($5.25\sigma$) through full-systematic OLS ($6.17\sigma$) to precision-weighted ($6.94\sigma$). Robustness checks — common-$\eta_{\rm resid}$ mixed model, leave-one-station-out meta-analysis, wild cluster bootstrap, phase-locked differential, cross-ephemeris validation on cleaned DE430 ($\eta_{\rm resid} = -7.04 \times 10^{-4}$, $5.96\sigma$; confirming the sign but differing in amplitude by $2.77\sigma$ from INPOP19a in the matched window), parametric GR-null bootstrap, and a frequency null scan — all support residual-channel survival of the synodic component (Section 4). If interpreted within the static Parametrized Post-Newtonian framework — an interpretation the residual-channel estimand does not yet license (Section 4.14) — the measured amplitude would correspond to $\beta_{\rm PPN} = 0.999902 \pm 1.07 \times 10^{-5}$ from the joint $(\beta_{\rm PPN}, \gamma)$ contour; this mapping is reported for scale only and is not advanced as a PPN measurement pending the integrator-level refit.

The result is therefore framed as a high-significance residual-channel candidate with a TEP interpretation, not as a completed replacement for direct-fit LLR bounds. The $6.94\sigma$ figure is a detection of a synodic pattern in post-fit residuals; it is not a measurement of the Nordtvedt parameter, and the quantitative relationship between $\eta_{\rm resid}$ and the direct-fit $\eta$ is not yet established. Source-level numerical refits of the INPOP or DE430 integrators with $\eta_{\rm resid}$ left free remain the critical open closure test. Ephemeris-absorption stress tests bound the residual-channel survival amplitude but do not replace a full dynamical integrator-level confirmation.
