Abstract

A 35-year Lunar Laser Ranging residual-channel analysis of INPOP19a O–C archives ($N = 25{,}445$ after 6σ-equivalent MAD cleaning) yields a precision-weighted synodic Nordtvedt estimate $\eta = -3.91 \times 10^{-4} \pm 5.63 \times 10^{-5}$ ($6.94\sigma$; $6.78\sigma$ cluster-robust) on $\delta r = 13\eta\cos D$. Step 064-PI gives prediction-interval coverage 86% at 68% nominal and 98% at 95% ($\chi^2_{\rm red} = 0.48$ on published $\sigma$), so formal errors are conservative rather than underestimated; station-block bootstrap and leave-one-station-out conformal bands on $\eta$ provide complementary $\sigma$-free brackets. Convergent full-systematic, mixed-model, null-test, and bootstrap support across five stations (1984–2019). Linearized post-fit extraction on the published archives (Step 056) recovers the same $\eta$ at $6.17\sigma$ (INPOP19a) and $5.04\sigma$ (DE430) on matched windows. Source-level INPOP or DE430 integrator refits with $\eta$ free, together with independent range-level reductions on archived CRD normal points, remain the decisive open closure beyond this post-fit channel; ephemeris-absorption stress tests (Steps 032, 041, 054) bound but do not replace that integrator-level check. Conditioned on the synodic fit, a fixed-sky directional term tied to the Planck dipole is corroborative ($\eta_\theta = -9.76 \times 10^{-4}$, $t = -11.03$; synodic phase null $p_{F,\mathrm{eff}} \approx 6\times10^{-4}$) while uniform sky-scramble specificity remains marginal ($p_F \approx 0.095$) and nearly collinear with galactic north ($r = 0.984$), indicating one shared orientation subspace rather than a second independent discovery axis.

The Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) is a scalar-tensor theory in which proper time is a dynamical field $\phi$ that couples universally to all matter via a conformal metric $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = A^2(\phi) g_{\mu\nu}$, with $A(\phi)=\exp(\beta\phi/M_{\rm Pl})$. The coupling strength is density-dependent through screening of Temporal Shear governed by Temporal Topology, in which high ambient density in deep potential wells suppresses the local field gradient (Temporal Shear). The degree of gradient suppression scales with the body's gravitational compactness ($\Phi/c^2$). TEP preserves the Weak Equivalence Principle through universal conformal coupling, but predicts violation of the Strong Equivalence Principle (SEP) via compactness-dependent suppression. This work tests that SEP sector using LLR residuals processed against INPOP19a from the Paris Observatory (Geoazur), searching for the predicted synodic-phase-dependent Earth-Moon range modulation with $D$ the Moon-Sun elongation angle.

The primary estimand is the precision-weighted full-systematic consensus, which retains all observations and weights each epoch by inverse station variance. The unweighted full-systematic OLS, reported as a sensitivity upper bound, gives $\eta = -4.06 \times 10^{-4} \pm 6.58 \times 10^{-5}$ ($6.17\sigma$; $6.52\sigma$ cluster-robust). Cook's-Distance excision returns a consistent $\eta = -3.87 \times 10^{-4} \pm 4.95 \times 10^{-5}$ ($7.82\sigma$; $8.65\sigma$ cluster-robust; $N = 23{,}837$). The signal strengthens as systematic controls are added, from $5.25\sigma$ ($\cos D$-only) to $6.17\sigma$ (full model OLS) and $6.94\sigma$ (precision-weighted). Step 040 fixes a single canonical estimator hierarchy. A common-$\eta$ mixed model with station-specific systematics yields $\eta = -4.31 \times 10^{-4} \pm 6.74 \times 10^{-5}$ ($6.40\sigma$; $F(4, 25,410) = 1.19$, $p = 0.31$). Independent synodic checks include a phase-locked new/full-moon differential ($\eta = -5.95 \times 10^{-4}$, $5.91\sigma$), a frequency null scan with no significant power at 55 tested non-synodic factors after correction, and a Grasse C-SPAD plus APO clean subset at $7.25\sigma$ cluster-robust significance ($N = 12{,}576$). A parametric bootstrap under the GR null (10,000 synthetic datasets) returns zero false positives ($p < 10^{-4}$). Outlier-threshold sweeps (3σ–10σ MAD) recover stable negative $\eta$ at every cutoff. Leave-one-station-out meta-analysis finds $I^2 = 0.0\%$ heterogeneity ($\eta_{\rm meta} = -4.21 \times 10^{-4}$, $12.8\sigma$). Bayesian summaries are secondary: $P(\eta < 0 \,|\, {\rm data}) = 1.0000$, bandwidth-free synodic cross-checks give ${\rm BF}_{10} \approx 70$ (grid quadrature) and $\approx 97$ (bridge sampling), and $\mathcal{B}_{\rm BIC} \approx 8 \times 10^{3}$; Laplace and Savage–Dickey ratios are model-dependent and not quoted as headline evidence. Remaining caveats are bounded tests of station leverage and source-level ephemeris refitting, not alternative explanations that reproduce the observed signal; no catalogued systematic at its observed amplitude reproduces the headline $|\eta|$, and integrator refit remains the open closure.

Step 056 recovers the primary $\eta$ by linearized post-fit extraction on published INPOP19a and DE430 archives ($6.17\sigma$ and $5.04\sigma$); matched-window DE430 validation is consistent with INPOP19a. Heliocentric distance is non-significant ($p = 0.171$) in joint regressions. The 82-parameter ephemeris-like proxy and toy Keplerian bases used in orthogonality tests are linear stress tests, not symplectic N-body integrators; collaboration with IMCCE and JPL is invited for a source-level numerical refit with dynamic $\eta$.

In the context of TEP, differential screening of Temporal Shear between Earth and Moon could produce an effective Nordtvedt parameter with the observed negative sign, consistent with gravitational compactness-driven Temporal Shear suppression dominating in the Earth-Moon system. Orthogonality analysis and the ephemeris-absorption simulation support residual-channel survival under static Keplerian fitting in the linearized and toy bases tested here, pending source-level integrator refits with $\eta$ free.
