Abstract

The Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) proposes that proper time is a dynamical scalar field $\phi$ rather than a geometric parameter. This field couples universally to all matter via a conformal metric $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = A^2(\phi)\,g_{\mu\nu}$. Temporal Shear is pinned where the ambient time-gradient is steep — weak-field language: $g \gg g_t \approx cH_0/2$ — by the environmental-state operator $\mathcal S_\Sigma(\mathcal E)$. The canonical pairwise benchmark gives an Earth–Moon response of order $10^{-14}$ and does not account for the reported range residual as a differential-fall signal; the analysis instead targets the synodic term in the post-fit residual channel, $\delta r = 13\eta_{\rm resid}\cos D$, where $D$ is the Moon-Sun elongation angle and $\eta_{\rm resid}$ is the residual-channel amplitude (an Observable Response Coefficient, distinct from the standard metric Nordtvedt parameter $\eta$). A candidate TEP interpretation is a clock/dynamics projection of the synodic field configuration rather than a screened force anomaly; its absolute transfer into passive two-way ranging is not established by the residual fit.

This work analyses 26,207 raw Lunar Laser Ranging O–C residuals from the public INPOP19a ephemeris archives (Paris Observatory, Geoazur), comprising $N = 25{,}445$ measurements from five international stations (1984–2019) after standard $6\sigma$ MAD outlier cleaning. The primary estimand is a precision-weighted synodic regression on the post-fit residual channel, with inverse station-variance weighting and a full systematic model with annual, monthly, and thermal $\cos(2D)$ regressors and a common intercept.

The primary precision-weighted residual-channel estimate is $\eta_{\rm resid} = -3.91 \times 10^{-4} \pm 5.63 \times 10^{-5}$ ($6.94\sigma$; $6.78\sigma$ cluster-robust). The amplitude stabilises in the band $-3.2$ to $-4.1 \times 10^{-4}$ across the estimator hierarchy, from cosD-only ($5.25\sigma$) through full-systematic OLS ($6.17\sigma$) to precision-weighted ($6.94\sigma$). Robustness checks — common-$\eta_{\rm resid}$ mixed model, leave-one-station-out meta-analysis, wild cluster bootstrap, phase-locked differential, cross-ephemeris validation on cleaned DE430 ($\eta_{\rm resid} = -7.04 \times 10^{-4}$, $5.96\sigma$; confirming the sign but differing in amplitude by $2.77\sigma$ from INPOP19a in the matched window), parametric GR-null bootstrap, and a frequency null scan — all support residual-channel survival of the synodic component (Section 4). If interpreted within the static Parametrized Post-Newtonian framework — an interpretation the residual-channel estimand does not yet license (Section 4.14) — the measured amplitude would correspond to $\beta_{\rm PPN} = 0.999902 \pm 1.07 \times 10^{-5}$ from the joint $(\beta_{\rm PPN}, \gamma)$ contour; this mapping is reported for scale only and is not advanced as a PPN measurement pending the integrator-level refit. A full-sky directional scan on the residual channel (2,664 right-ascension/declination grid points at 5° intervals) places the Planck CMB dipole axis at rank 226/2664 (top 8.5%).

The result is therefore framed as a high-significance residual-channel candidate with a TEP interpretation, not as a completed replacement for direct-fit LLR bounds. The $6.94\sigma$ figure is a detection of a synodic pattern in post-fit residuals; it is not a measurement of the Nordtvedt parameter, and the quantitative relationship between $\eta_{\rm resid}$ and the direct-fit $\eta$ is not yet established. Source-level numerical refits of the INPOP or DE430 integrators with $\eta_{\rm resid}$ left free remain the critical open closure test. Ephemeris-absorption stress tests bound the residual-channel survival amplitude but do not replace a full dynamical integrator-level confirmation.
