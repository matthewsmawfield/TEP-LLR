2. Theoretical Framework: TEP and the Nordtvedt Effect

The Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) is a scalar-tensor theory in which proper time becomes a dynamical field $\phi=\bar\phi+M_{\rm Pl}u$ that couples to the local mass density. In this framework, matter couples to the canonical unified metric $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = -A_{\rm loc}^2(u_{\rm grav}(\mathbf x))U_\mu U_\nu+A_{\rm sp}^2(u_{\rm drift}(t,\mathbf x))\Pi_{\mu\nu} + B(\phi)\Pi_\mu^\alpha\Pi_\nu^\beta\nabla_\alpha\phi\nabla_\beta\phi$, where $A_{\rm sp}(u_{\rm drift}) = \exp(\beta_A u_{\rm drift})=\exp(-u_{\rm drift})$ is the cosmological spatial ruler factor and $A_{\rm loc}(u_{\rm grav})=\exp(\beta_A u_{\rm grav})$ rescales the local $(3+1)$ ADM lapse along $U_\mu\equiv-\nabla_\mu\bar\phi/\sqrt{2\bar X}$ at unstretched spatial 3-metric $\tilde\gamma_{ij}=\gamma_{ij}$ at fixed cosmic epoch ($A_{\rm sp}(t_0)=1$; Paper 0, §2.2, Step 80). The rate at which proper time accumulates depends on the local value of $\phi$, which in turn is determined by the matter sources, potential, kinetic response and boundary conditions. The nonlinear kinetic operator suppresses the gradient response where the ambient time-gradient is steep (weak-field: high-acceleration configurations); ambient density and compactness are diagnostics of that configuration, not additional microscopic screening laws.

Screening projection notice. Screening in TEP is represented at theory level by the environmental operator S_Σ(E). Quantities such as ρ_T, R_T(M), S_⊕(r), compactness Φ/c^2, local stellar density, thermal epoch, coherence length, proximity, and boundary geometry are domain-specific projections of E, not independent screening mechanisms and not interchangeable universal thresholds.

2.1 The Nordtvedt Effect

In scalar-tensor theories, bodies with different gravitational binding energy per unit mass will fall at different rates in an external gravitational field. This is the Nordtvedt effect, first derived by Kenneth Nordtvedt (Nordtvedt 1968) as a test of the Strong Equivalence Principle. The Nordtvedt parameter $\eta$ quantifies the strength of SEP violation:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:nordtvedt_param} \eta = 4\beta - \gamma - 3 - \frac{10}{3}\xi - \alpha_1 + \frac{2}{3}\alpha_2 - \frac{2}{3}\zeta_1 - \frac{1}{3}\zeta_2 \end{equation}

where $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are the post-Newtonian parameters, $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ are preferred-frame parameters, $\xi$ is the Whitehead parameter, and $\zeta_1$, $\zeta_2$ are conservation-law parameters. In General Relativity ($\beta = \gamma = 1$, all others zero), $\eta = 0$ exactly. In standard scalar-tensor theories such as Brans-Dicke, the Nordtvedt parameter is approximately $\eta_{\rm BD} \approx 2\alpha_0^2/(1+\alpha_0^2)$ where $\alpha_0 = \sqrt{2}\,\beta_A$ is the DEF-normalised bare scalar-tensor coupling (following the corpus-wide convention $\varphi = \phi/M_{\rm Pl}$, so that $\beta_A = d\ln A/d\varphi$ is dimensionless; in the unscreened TEP limit $\beta_A = -1$, giving $\alpha_0 = -\sqrt{2}$). An illustrative effective Earth–Moon coupling $\alpha_{\rm eff}^{(\oplus-{\rm Moon})} \approx 3 \times 10^{-3}$ is adopted for the phenomenological estimate below. Two readings of that scale are kept distinct throughout: as a screened-body coupling it enters the fall differential only in product with the solar source charge $| \alpha_0\,S_\Sigma^{(\odot)} | \lesssim 1.6\times10^{-5}$ ($8.2\times10^{-6}$ on the isotropic benchmark) bounded by Cassini; as a full observable response coefficient it already contains that factor. The bookkeeping below shows that only the second reading can stand at the quoted amplitude — which makes the volumetric number an effective response coefficient, not a decomposable force estimate.

The fitted $\eta_{\rm resid}$ used throughout the remainder of this paper is a post-fit residual-channel proxy extracted from published INPOP19a and DE430 O–C residuals. It is not a direct replacement for ephemeris-level SEP bounds until the TEP/Nordtvedt parameter is included in the source-level dynamical integrator. The symbol $\eta$ without a subscript denotes the standard Nordtvedt parameter defined in Eq. ($\ref{eq:nordtvedt_param}$), while $\eta_{\rm resid}$ denotes the empirically extracted residual-channel amplitude reported in Sections 3–5.

A Nordtvedt effect tests differential response associated with gravitational self-binding; it is distinct from the fitted synodic residual. TEP's universal conformal coupling $A(\phi)$ preserves the Weak Equivalence Principle (all non-gravitational processes couple to the same matter metric $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}$, consistent with the TEP framework paper, Paper 0, §7.2). However, the Strong Equivalence Principle may be violated through the suppression of Temporal Shear.

The operative shear response is obtained from the nonlinear kinetic field configuration and its source/body matching. Surface compactness $\Phi/c^2=GM/Rc^2$ is an environmental diagnostic used in the historical estimates below, not an independent microscopic screening law. Neither a suppressed gradient nor a fitted residual coefficient is by itself a measurement of a body's conserved source charge.

The compactness-squared and volumetric constructions below are retained as historical and phenomenological checks. They are not derivations of the operative kinetic operator or of the measured $\eta_{\rm resid}$. The canonical response and the coupled-field diagnostic are evaluated separately below; the empirical residual analysis does not depend on adopting the historical compactness model.

For two bodies with different compactness (Earth and Moon), the differential acceleration in an external gravitational field $a_0$ produces a Nordtvedt-like range modulation. In an unscreened toy baseline scaling simply with surface compactness, the differential coupling would be:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:effective_coupling_diff} \Delta\alpha_{\rm eff} = \alpha_{\rm eff,\oplus} - \alpha_{\rm eff,{\rm Moon}} \approx \alpha_0 \left[ \left(\frac{\Phi}{c^2}\right)_\oplus - \left(\frac{\Phi}{c^2}\right)_{\rm Moon} \right] \end{equation}

Substituting this naive compactness scaling and defining the residual-channel parameter $\eta_{\rm resid}$ (the TEP-specific residual amplitude, distinct from the standard Nordtvedt parameter $\eta_N = 2\alpha_0^2/(1+\alpha_0^2)$, which vanishes under screening) yields the toy baseline:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:tep_prediction} \eta_{\rm resid} \approx \frac{\delta a}{a_0} \approx \alpha_0^2 \left[ \left(\frac{\Phi_\oplus}{c^2}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{\Phi_{\rm Moon}}{c^2}\right)^2 \right] \end{equation}

where $\Phi_{\oplus}/c^2 \approx 7 \times 10^{-10}$ is Earth's compactness and $\Phi_{\rm Moon}/c^2 \approx 3 \times 10^{-11}$ is the Moon's compactness. As established below (Section 2.1), this naive surface-compactness baseline yields $\eta_{\rm resid} \sim 10^{-18}$ at the bare coupling — and $\lesssim 3\times10^{-29}$ once the screened solar source charge is folded in — and serves only as a null check confirming that bare surface compactness cannot drive the LLR anomaly. The negative response assigned by the volumetric model in Eq. ($\ref{eq:volumetric_suppression}$) is a property of that phenomenological construction, not a separately established sign prediction of the kinetic field-to-range map.

Second, in the Damour–Esposito-Farèse (DEF) scalar-tensor framework the Nordtvedt parameter is linear in the sensitivity difference:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:eta_def_standard} \eta_{\rm DEF} = \frac{4\alpha_0^2\,(s_\oplus - s_{\rm Moon})}{1 + \alpha_0^2} , \end{equation}

where $s_\oplus$ and $s_{\rm Moon}$ are the gravitational self-energies (sensitivities) of Earth and the Moon. The TEP-specific volumetric model adopted here uses a different functional form that integrates interior density profiles against the saturation scale $\rho_T$:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:eta_from_alpha_eff} \eta \approx \alpha_{\rm eff,\oplus}^2 - \alpha_{\rm eff,{\rm Moon}}^2 . \end{equation}

Substituting Eq. ($\ref{eq:effective_coupling_diff}$) into Eq. ($\ref{eq:eta_from_alpha_eff}$) yields the TEP prediction in Eq. ($\ref{eq:tep_prediction}$). Earth's deeper potential well flattens Temporal Topology more strongly than the Moon's, suppressing Temporal Shear and yielding a significantly smaller effective coupling.

Two theoretically distinct estimates are evaluated. The compactness-squared expression above uses only the surface compactness differential, evaluated with $(\alpha_0 S_\Sigma^{(\odot)})^2$ as the effective squared coupling. Even at the Cassini ceiling $|\alpha_0 S_\Sigma^{(\odot)}| \lesssim 1.6\times10^{-5}$ ($S_\Sigma^{(\odot)} \lesssim 1.15\times10^{-5}$ under the unified unstretched-space metric, or $5.8\times10^{-6}$ on the isotropic benchmark, with $\alpha_0 = \sqrt{2}\,\beta_A = -\sqrt{2}$; Paper 0, Step 80), and $\Delta(\Phi^2/c^4) \approx 5\times10^{-19}$, this gives $\eta_{\rm resid} \approx \alpha_0^2 S_\Sigma^{(\odot)\,2}\,\Delta(\Phi^2/c^4) \lesssim (1.6\times10^{-5})^2\times5\times10^{-19} \approx 1.3\times10^{-28}$ ($3\times10^{-29}$ on the isotropic benchmark) — essentially zero. This baseline is not a physical prediction for LLR; it is a consistency check that confirms the measured $\eta_{\rm resid}$ cannot arise from the bare coupling alone. $\eta_{\rm resid}$ is an emergent observable response coefficient, not a microscopic parameter.

The phenomenological TEP prediction is obtained from the volumetric suppression model, which integrates interior density profiles rather than surface compactness:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:volumetric_suppression} \eta_{\rm resid} \approx \alpha_{\rm eff}^{(\oplus-{\rm Moon})} \left( \langle(\rho/\rho_T)^3\rangle_\oplus - \langle(\rho/\rho_T)^3\rangle_{\rm Moon} \right) \end{equation}

Using physical parameters from the TEP framework paper (Paper 0, Jakarta v0.16): the canonical bare scalar-tensor coupling $\alpha_0 = -\sqrt{2}$ (DEF normalisation, $\alpha_0 = \sqrt{2}\,\beta_A$ with $\beta_A = -1$), an illustrative effective Earth–Moon coupling $\alpha_{\rm eff}^{(\oplus-{\rm Moon})} \approx 3 \times 10^{-3}$, and a simplified PREM density model with $\rho_T \approx 20$ g/cm³ from GNSS calibration (Paper 6) yielding an Earth-Moon shielding differential $\Delta\langle(\rho/\rho_T)^3\rangle \approx 3.22\times10^{-2}$. The phenomenological volumetric model prediction is therefore $\eta_{\rm resid} \approx (-3 \times 10^{-3}) \times (3.22 \times 10^{-2}) \approx -9.7 \times 10^{-5}$, agreeing in sign and order of magnitude but underestimating the measured headline value by a factor of approximately four; the magnitude match is contingent on the illustrative $\alpha_{\rm eff}$ and is not a test of the theory. This phenomenological volumetric model supplies a negative response in the $10^{-4}$ regime under its assumed weighting and normalization. It does not yet provide a first-principles, parameter-free prediction of $\eta_{\rm resid}$. The headline precision-weighted estimate $\eta_{\rm resid} = -3.91\times10^{-4}$ and the naïve OLS robustness check $\eta_{\rm resid} = -3.18\times10^{-4}$ lie in the same order-of-magnitude regime, but the phenomenological model uncertainties — PREM simplification, the exponent in $(\rho/\rho_T)^3$, $\rho_T = 20 \pm 7$ g/cm³, and the illustrative nature of the $\alpha_{\rm eff}^{(\oplus-{\rm Moon})}$ normalization — preclude a precision comparison at this stage.

Step 83 separates the historical volumetric weighting from the equilibrium-depth relation supplied by the potential sector (Paper 0 v0.16, Appendix E). The quartic matter-branch potential pins the equilibrium interior level as $\phi_{\min}(\rho) \propto \rho^{1/3}$ — the derived density response is the cube root, not the cube — supplying the depth-bookkeeping factor $S_A = \min[1,(\bar\rho/\rho_T)^{1/3}]$: $S_A^{(\oplus)} = 0.650$ ($0.43$–$0.88$ across the $\rho_T$ band) at $\bar\rho_\oplus = 5.49$ g/cm³ versus $S_A^{(\rm Moon)} = 0.550$ ($0.36$–$0.75$ across the $\rho_T$ band) at $\bar\rho_{\rm Moon} = 3.33$ g/cm³, an equilibrium-depth differential $\Delta S_A=0.100$, not a replacement for the illustrative $\alpha_{\rm eff}$ in a force or range equation. Volume-integrated over the same PREM-style profiles, the derived pinning differential $\Delta\langle(\rho/\rho_T)^{1/3}\rangle = 8.8\times10^{-2}$ likewise supersedes the phenomenological $\Delta\langle(\rho/\rho_T)^3\rangle = 3.22\times10^{-2}$ (which over-concentrates the weighting on the core). The corrected static boundary calculation in Paper 0, Appendix E R17, separates infinitesimal susceptibilities from finite-excursion responses and does not establish a universal lunar transmission. This adjudication is a consistency check the closure passes rather than a new contradiction: this retained unit-term pairwise control predicts a dynamical Nordtvedt contribution below the stated bound; it is not a selected-action finite-source prediction, in line with the tight direct-fit LLR bounds on differential fall. The measured $\cos(D)$ residual — independently present in the published ephemeris record — is not explained by this pairwise force projection. 2.2 Predicted Signal in LLR

The residual-channel estimand is extracted on the standard synodic basis: a fitted modulation of the Earth-Moon O–C range residuals as the Earth-Moon system orbits the Sun, of the form:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:range_perturbation} \delta r = 13 \eta_{\rm resid} \cos(D) \end{equation}

The predicted amplitude scales linearly: $|\delta r| = 13 |\eta_{\rm resid}|$ meters. For the order-of-magnitude estimate $\eta_{\rm resid} \sim 10^{-4}$, this gives $\sim$1.3 mm. The headline precision-weighted estimate ($\eta_{\rm resid} = -3.91 \times 10^{-4}$, Section 4.1) predicts an amplitude of $\sim$5.1 mm, while the leverage-excised robustness check ($\eta_{\rm resid} \approx -3.6 \times 10^{-4}$) predicts $\sim$4.7 mm and the naïve cosD-only OLS robustness check ($\eta_{\rm resid} \approx -3.18 \times 10^{-4}$) yields $\sim$4.13 mm. These amplitudes are small compared to the centimetre-level residual RMS, but with 26,207 observations over 35 years, statistical averaging provides sensitivity well below the per-observation noise floor.

2.3 Continuous Gradient Suppression and the Saturation Scale

The operative v0.16 shear-screening mechanism is the nonlinear kinetic response $P_{,X}$ of the solved scalar gradient. Density, compactness and $\rho_T$ are environmental diagnostics, not independent microscopic screening laws. The canonical Earth–Moon pairwise benchmark is strongly suppressed. Step 086's coupled syzygy diagnostic replaces the earlier matched-profile bridge estimate for its stated source geometry; it does not itself perform an ephemeris-level range fit. Historical compactness and volumetric checks are retained separately from this kinetic result.

The Earth–Moon system is embedded in the solar and Galactic field, so interplanetary density alone does not fix its response. The following heliocentric modulation is an empirical residual-channel template, not an independently derived Nordtvedt force law:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:orbital_modulation} \eta_{\rm resid}(r_\odot) = \eta_0 \left( 1 + m \cdot \frac{r_{\rm mean} - r_\odot}{r_{\rm mean} \cdot e_\oplus} \right) \end{equation}

where $\eta_0$ is the baseline residual-channel coefficient at mean orbital distance $r_{\rm mean}$, $e_\oplus \approx 0.0167$ is Earth's orbital eccentricity, and $m$ is the modulation depth characterizing the nonlinear response. For $m \approx 1$ (consistent with the observed sign-flip), the orbital modulation generates diagnostic sidebands at frequencies $D \pm l'$ (where $l'$ is the orbital longitude), spectrally orthogonal to classical multi-body resonances. This provides a unique signature distinguishing TEP from standard PPN or systematic artifacts.

The TEP framework further predicts that the coupling depends not only on the scalar gradient magnitude (distance) but also on the rate at which the Earth-Moon system traverses the temporal topology. A body moving through a spatial scalar gradient $\nabla\phi$ at velocity $\mathbf{v}$ experiences an effective temporal shear rate $d\phi/dt \approx \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla\phi \approx v_r \, |\nabla\phi|$, where $v_r$ is the heliocentric radial velocity. For a Kepler orbit with small eccentricity, distance $r$ and radial velocity $v_r$ are approximately in quadrature ($90^\circ$ out of phase), making them statistically distinguishable predictors. The full dynamical modulation therefore takes the form:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:velocity_modulation} \eta_{\rm resid}(r_\odot, v_r) = \eta_0 \left( 1 + m_r \cdot \frac{r_{\rm mean} - r_\odot}{r_{\rm mean} \cdot e_\oplus} + m_v \cdot \frac{v_r}{\bar{v}_r} \right) \end{equation}

where $\bar{v}_r$ is the characteristic radial velocity scale ($\approx 0.5$ km/s for Earth's orbit) and $m_v$ is the velocity modulation depth. A joint fit to both $r_\odot$ and $v_r$ determines whether the temporal topology is purely static ($m_v = 0$) or dynamically responsive ($m_v \neq 0$). The heliocentric radial velocity analysis tests this prediction directly, using DE440 ephemeris velocities computed via Skyfield for every LLR observation epoch.

A further TEP-motivated probe uses a fixed celestial axis tied to the Planck 2018 dipole as an operational reference: $(l, b) = (264.02^\circ, 48.25^\circ)$ in galactic coordinates, equivalent to $(\alpha, \delta) = (168.14^\circ, -7.22^\circ)$ in J2000 equatorial coordinates, with amplitude $v_{\rm CMB} \approx 369$ km/s for the kinematic dipole. Some scalar-field embeddings single out a large-scale rest frame; the LLR regressions reported here do not, by themselves, establish Lorentz violation or uniqueness of that frame. They test whether residual-channel structure is consistent with anisotropic coupling defined relative to $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\rm CMB}$ and with other fixed axes used as controls (Section 4.25.2). Two operational predictions tied to $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\rm CMB}$ are:

First, an annual velocity projection: Earth's orbital velocity ($\sim 30$ km/s) projects onto the CMB dipole direction with a sinusoidally varying parallel component $v_\parallel(t) = \mathbf{v}_{\rm orb} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\rm CMB}$. The CMB dipole lies at ecliptic longitude $\approx 173^\circ$, offset by approximately $70^\circ$ from the perihelion longitude ($\approx 103^\circ$), making the annual velocity projection phase-shifted relative to the heliocentric distance modulation and therefore statistically distinguishable.

Second, a monthly orientation anisotropy: the Earth-Moon line sweeps across the celestial sphere with synodic period. If an effective gradient projection onto $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\rm CMB}$ is present in the same channel, the coupling should depend on the cosine of the angle $\theta$ between the Earth-Moon vector and that axis:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:cmb_anisotropy} \eta_{\rm resid} = \eta_0 \left( 1 + m_{\rm CMB} \cdot \cos\theta_{\rm EM-CMB} \right) \end{equation}

where $\cos\theta_{\rm EM-CMB} = \hat{\mathbf{r}}_{\rm EM} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\rm CMB}$. Because the Earth-Moon line rotates on a monthly timescale while the synodic signal operates on a 29.5-day period, the two modulations are at different frequencies and can be separated in a joint fit. The CMB dipole orientation analysis tests both predictions directly.

Potential curvature contributes to the perturbation operator, but a density-dependent mass does not replace the kinetic response as an independent Earth–Moon screening prescription. Compactness diagnostics and the empirical modulation templates above do not establish either the absolute force or the clock-to-range transfer of the measured term.

2.4 The Secular Channel and the $\dot G/G$ Bound

Dedicated LLR fits constrain the present local dynamical observable $|\dot G/G|\lesssim4\times10^{-13}\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$ (Williams et al.). If the scalar-response factor and the gravitational-to-atomic unit conversion are held fixed, $G_{\rm loc}=G_*A^2(1+\alpha^2)$ gives the conditional relation $\dot G/G\simeq2\,d\ln A/dt$ and the corresponding local scale $|\dot A/A|\lesssim2\times10^{-13}\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$. Conversion to an ambient-field history requires the actual source, body and time-dependent measurement response. Paper 0, R17 supplies a static boundary diagnostic, not that cosmological transfer. The present bound is therefore not extrapolated into a cosmological lookback constraint or used to assign redshift uniquely to an emission-epoch spatial contrast (Paper 0, §8).
