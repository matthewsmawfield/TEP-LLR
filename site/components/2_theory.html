2. Theoretical Framework: TEP and the Nordtvedt Effect

The Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) is a scalar-tensor theory in which proper time becomes a dynamical field $\phi$ that couples to the local mass density. In this framework, matter couples to a conformal metric $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = A^2(\phi) g_{\mu\nu} + B(\phi)\nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi$, where $A(\phi) = \exp(\beta_A\phi/M_{\rm Pl})$ is the conformal factor. The rate at which proper time accumulates depends on the local value of $\phi$, which in turn depends on the ambient matter density through screening of Temporal Shear. This operates via the continuous spatial profile of the time field (Temporal Topology), in which high ambient density in deep potential wells suppresses the local field gradient (Temporal Shear), naturally attenuating Temporal Shear effects in dense environments while allowing the field to remain light and long-ranged in dilute regions.

Screening projection notice. Screening in TEP is represented at theory level by the environmental operator S_Σ(E). Quantities such as ρ_T, R_T(M), S_⊕(r), compactness Φ/c^2, local stellar density, thermal epoch, coherence length, proximity, and boundary geometry are domain-specific projections of E, not independent screening mechanisms and not interchangeable universal thresholds.

2.1 The Nordtvedt Effect

In scalar-tensor theories, bodies with different gravitational binding energy per unit mass will fall at different rates in an external gravitational field. This is the Nordtvedt effect, first derived by Kenneth Nordtvedt (Nordtvedt 1968) as a test of the Strong Equivalence Principle. The Nordtvedt parameter $\eta$ quantifies the strength of SEP violation:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:nordtvedt_param} \eta = 4\beta - \gamma - 3 - \frac{10}{3}\xi - \alpha_1 + \frac{2}{3}\alpha_2 - \frac{2}{3}\zeta_1 - \frac{1}{3}\zeta_2 \end{equation}

where $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are the post-Newtonian parameters, $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ are preferred-frame parameters, $\xi$ is the Whitehead parameter, and $\zeta_1$, $\zeta_2$ are conservation-law parameters. In General Relativity ($\beta = \gamma = 1$, all others zero), $\eta = 0$ exactly. In standard scalar-tensor theories such as Brans-Dicke, the Nordtvedt parameter is approximately $\eta_{\rm BD} \approx 2\alpha_0^2/(1+\alpha_0^2)$ where $\alpha_0 = \sqrt{2}\,\beta_A$ is the DEF-normalised bare scalar-tensor coupling (following the corpus-wide convention $\varphi = \phi/M_{\rm Pl}$, so that $\beta_A = d\ln A/d\varphi$ is dimensionless; in the unscreened TEP limit $\beta_A = -1$, giving $\alpha_0 = -\sqrt{2}$). An illustrative effective Earth--Moon coupling $\alpha_{\rm eff}^{(\oplus-{\rm Moon})} \approx 3 \times 10^{-3}$ is adopted for the phenomenological estimate below. Two readings of that scale are kept distinct throughout: as a screened-body coupling it enters the fall differential only in product with the solar source charge $| \alpha_0\,S_\Sigma^{(\odot)} | \lesssim 8\times10^{-6}$ bounded by Cassini; as a full observable response coefficient it already contains that factor. The bookkeeping below shows that only the second reading can stand at the quoted amplitude — which makes the volumetric number an effective response coefficient, not a decomposable force estimate.

The fitted $\eta_{\rm resid}$ used throughout the remainder of this paper is a post-fit residual-channel proxy extracted from published INPOP19a and DE430 O–C residuals. It is not a direct replacement for ephemeris-level SEP bounds until the TEP/Nordtvedt parameter is included in the source-level dynamical integrator. The symbol $\eta$ without a subscript denotes the standard Nordtvedt parameter defined in Eq.~(\ref{eq:nordtvedt_param}), while $\eta_{\rm resid}$ denotes the empirically extracted residual-channel amplitude reported in Sections 3–5.

In TEP, the Nordtvedt effect arises from a compactness-dependent scalar coupling. TEP's universal conformal coupling $A(\phi)$ preserves the Weak Equivalence Principle (all non-gravitational processes couple to the same matter metric $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}$, consistent with the TEP framework paper, Paper 0, §7.2). However, the Strong Equivalence Principle may be violated through the suppression of Temporal Shear.

For a self-gravitating body in the deeply suppressed regime, the effective scalar coupling is suppressed relative to the bare coupling $\alpha_0$ as Temporal Shear vanishes in the deep potential well. The suppression of Temporal Shear (vanishing field gradient) reduces the effective coupling to $\alpha_{\rm eff} \ll \alpha_0$. The degree of gradient suppression scales with the body's surface gravitational compactness $\Phi/c^2 = GM/Rc^2$ (see the TEP framework paper, Paper 0, §7, for a detailed discussion of the relationship between compactness and gradient suppression).

The compactness-dependent gradient suppression that generates the Nordtvedt effect in this analysis serves as the strongly screened, Solar-System realisation of the abstract environmental operator $\mathcal{S}_\Sigma(\mathcal{E})$. In this deeply embedded regime, the Earth-Moon compactness differential acts as the empirical proxy for the continuous saturation of Temporal Topology, yielding a heavily attenuated observable response coefficient $\eta_{\rm resid}$ without committing to specific chameleon or symmetron microphysics.

For two bodies with different compactness (Earth and Moon), the differential acceleration in an external gravitational field $a_0$ produces a Nordtvedt-like range modulation. In an unscreened toy baseline scaling simply with surface compactness, the differential coupling would be:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:effective_coupling_diff} \Delta\alpha_{\rm eff} = \alpha_{\rm eff,\oplus} - \alpha_{\rm eff,{\rm Moon}} \approx \alpha_0 \left[ \left(\frac{\Phi}{c^2}\right)_\oplus - \left(\frac{\Phi}{c^2}\right)_{\rm Moon} \right] \end{equation}

Substituting this naive compactness scaling and defining the residual-channel parameter $\eta_{\rm resid}$ (the TEP-specific residual amplitude, distinct from the standard Nordtvedt parameter $\eta_N = 2\alpha_0^2/(1+\alpha_0^2)$, which vanishes under screening) yields the toy baseline:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:tep_prediction} \eta_{\rm resid} \approx \frac{\delta a}{a_0} \approx \alpha_0^2 \left[ \left(\frac{\Phi_\oplus}{c^2}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{\Phi_{\rm Moon}}{c^2}\right)^2 \right] \end{equation}

where $\Phi_{\oplus}/c^2 \approx 7 \times 10^{-10}$ is Earth's compactness and $\Phi_{\rm Moon}/c^2 \approx 3 \times 10^{-11}$ is the Moon's compactness. As established below (Section 2.1), this naive surface-compactness baseline yields $\eta_{\rm resid} \sim 10^{-18}$ at the bare coupling — and $\lesssim 3\times10^{-29}$ once the screened solar source charge is folded in — and serves only as a null check confirming that bare surface compactness cannot drive the LLR anomaly. In the physical TEP framework, Earth's deeper potential well flattens Temporal Topology more strongly than the Moon's, so Temporal Shear screening suppresses Earth's effective coupling relative to the Moon's ($\alpha_{\rm eff,\oplus} < \alpha_{\rm eff,{\rm Moon}}$), naturally generating a negative differential response ($\eta_{\rm resid} < 0$) that is evaluated quantitatively via the volumetric suppression model in Eq.~(\ref{eq:volumetric_suppression}).

Second, in the Damour–Esposito-Farèse (DEF) scalar-tensor framework the Nordtvedt parameter is linear in the sensitivity difference:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:eta_def_standard} \eta_{\rm DEF} = \frac{4\alpha_0^2\,(s_\oplus - s_{\rm Moon})}{1 + \alpha_0^2} , \end{equation}

where $s_\oplus$ and $s_{\rm Moon}$ are the gravitational self-energies (sensitivities) of Earth and the Moon. The TEP-specific volumetric model adopted here uses a different functional form that integrates interior density profiles against the saturation scale $\rho_T$:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:eta_from_alpha_eff} \eta \approx \alpha_{\rm eff,\oplus}^2 - \alpha_{\rm eff,{\rm Moon}}^2 . \end{equation}

Substituting Eq.~(\ref{eq:effective_coupling_diff}) into Eq.~(\ref{eq:eta_from_alpha_eff}) yields the TEP prediction in Eq.~(\ref{eq:tep_prediction}). Earth's deeper potential well flattens Temporal Topology more strongly than the Moon's, suppressing Temporal Shear and yielding a significantly smaller effective coupling.

Two theoretically distinct estimates are evaluated. The compactness-squared expression above uses only the surface compactness differential, evaluated with $(\alpha_0 S_\Sigma^{(\odot)})^2$ as the effective squared coupling. Even at the Cassini ceiling $|\alpha_0 S_\Sigma^{(\odot)}| \lesssim 8.2\times10^{-6}$ ($S_\Sigma^{(\odot)} \lesssim 5.8\times10^{-6}$ with $\alpha_0 = \sqrt{2}\,\beta_A = -\sqrt{2}$), and $\Delta(\Phi^2/c^4) \approx 5\times10^{-19}$, this gives $\eta_{\rm resid} \approx \alpha_0^2 S_\Sigma^{(\odot)\,2}\,\Delta(\Phi^2/c^4) \lesssim (8.2\times10^{-6})^2\times5\times10^{-19} \approx 3\times10^{-29}$ — essentially zero. This baseline is not a physical prediction for LLR; it is a consistency check that confirms the measured $\eta_{\rm resid}$ cannot arise from the bare coupling alone. $\eta_{\rm resid}$ is an emergent observable response coefficient, not a microscopic parameter.

The phenomenological TEP prediction is obtained from the volumetric suppression model, which integrates interior density profiles rather than surface compactness:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:volumetric_suppression} \eta_{\rm resid} \approx \alpha_{\rm eff}^{(\oplus-{\rm Moon})} \left( \langle(\rho/\rho_T)^3\rangle_\oplus - \langle(\rho/\rho_T)^3\rangle_{\rm Moon} \right) \end{equation}

Using physical parameters from the TEP framework paper (Paper 0, Jakarta v0.14): the canonical bare scalar-tensor coupling $\alpha_0 = -\sqrt{2}$ (DEF normalisation, $\alpha_0 = \sqrt{2}\,\beta_A$ with $\beta_A = -1$), an illustrative effective Earth--Moon coupling $\alpha_{\rm eff}^{(\oplus-{\rm Moon})} \approx 3 \times 10^{-3}$, and a simplified PREM density model with $\rho_T \approx 20$ g/cm³ from GNSS calibration (Paper 6) yielding an Earth-Moon shielding differential $\Delta\langle(\rho/\rho_T)^3\rangle \approx 3.22\times10^{-2}$. The phenomenological volumetric model prediction is therefore $\eta_{\rm resid} \approx (-3 \times 10^{-3}) \times (3.22 \times 10^{-2}) \approx -9.7 \times 10^{-5}$, agreeing in sign and order of magnitude but underestimating the measured headline value by a factor of approximately four; the magnitude match is contingent on the illustrative $\alpha_{\rm eff}$ and is not a test of the theory. The current TEP framework robustly predicts the sign of the effect under compactness-driven Temporal Shear screening and gives an order-of-magnitude expectation in the $10^{-4}$ regime under this phenomenological volumetric model. It does not yet provide a first-principles, parameter-free prediction of $\eta_{\rm resid}$. The headline precision-weighted estimate $\eta_{\rm resid} = -3.91\times10^{-4}$ and the naïve OLS robustness check $\eta_{\rm resid} = -3.18\times10^{-4}$ lie in the same order-of-magnitude regime, but the phenomenological model uncertainties — PREM simplification, the exponent in $(\rho/\rho_T)^3$, $\rho_T = 20 \pm 7$ g/cm³, and the illustrative nature of the $\alpha_{\rm eff}^{(\oplus-{\rm Moon})}$ normalization — preclude a precision comparison at this stage.

Both underived ingredients are superseded by the corpus's master-action machinery (Paper 0 v0.15, Appendix E), computed in step 83. The quartic matter-branch potential pins the interior field as $\phi_{\min}(\rho) \propto \rho^{1/3}$ — the derived density response is the cube root, not the cube — and the amplitude sector supplies the zero-parameter interior factor $S_A = \min[1,(\bar\rho/\rho_T)^{1/3}]$: $S_A^{(\oplus)} = 0.650$ at $\bar\rho_\oplus = 5.49$ g/cm³ versus $S_A^{(\rm Moon)} = 0.550$ at $\bar\rho_{\rm Moon} = 3.33$ g/cm³, a derived differential $\Delta S_A = 0.100$ replacing the illustrative $\alpha_{\rm eff}$. Volume-integrated over the same PREM-style profiles, the derived pinning differential $\Delta\langle(\rho/\rho_T)^{1/3}\rangle = 8.8\times10^{-2}$ likewise supersedes the phenomenological $\Delta\langle(\rho/\rho_T)^3\rangle = 3.22\times10^{-2}$ (which over-concentrates the weighting on the core). The admissible channel then carries the computed lunar excursion $\delta\phi_\odot = 2.54\times10^{-11}\cos D$, attenuated by the Moon's own amplitude factor to $\delta\phi_{\rm eff} = S_A^{(\rm Moon)}\delta\phi_\odot \simeq 1.4\times10^{-11}$, whose direct clock-projection ceiling $\delta r = (c\,\tau_{\rm rt}/2)\,\delta\phi_{\rm eff} \simeq 5.4$ mm brackets the measured $5.1$ mm modulation — the required ephemeris transfer fraction is order unity, consistent in order of magnitude, with the explicit ephemeris-fit map (which also fixes the residual's sign through the projection convention) the remaining channel-level derivation.

Source-charge bookkeeping. Read as a mediated-force estimate, the volumetric number is not internally consistent on the corpus's own terms. A differential-fall signature carries both vertices of the interaction, $\delta a/a_0 = (\alpha_0 S_\Sigma^{(\odot)})\,\Delta\alpha_{\rm body}$: the screened solar charge sourcing the field and the screened body coupling responding to it. Folding the Cassini-bounded source charge into the same bookkeeping returns $\eta_{\rm resid} \lesssim (8.2\times10^{-6})\times(3\times10^{-3})\times(3.22\times10^{-2}) \approx 8\times10^{-10}$ when $\alpha_{\rm eff}$ is read as a body coupling — and $\lesssim 2.4\times10^{-8}$ when it is read as the entire body-charge differential — at least four orders of magnitude below the measured band in either reading. The quoted $-9.7\times10^{-5}$ therefore admits no charge$\times$charge decomposition: it can only be an effective response coefficient in which the source factor has been absorbed rather than eliminated. This bookkeeping failure excludes the force-channel reading independently of, and prior to, the operator-level suppression below.

A compactness-differential force anomaly of the kind the volumetric model describes is thereby suppressed to the $10^{-14}$–$10^{-15}$ level — invisible in this channel — and the illustrative $\alpha_{\rm eff}^{(\oplus-{\rm Moon})}$ normalization cannot be carried forward under the derived operator. This adjudication is a consistency check the closure passes rather than a new contradiction: the canonical pairwise projection predicts a dynamical Nordtvedt contribution below the stated bound, in line with the tight direct-fit LLR bounds on differential fall. The measured $\cos(D)$ residual — independently present in the published ephemeris record — is not explained by this pairwise force projection. The conformal excursion $\delta\phi_\odot(t)$ is a one-point function of the temporal landscape evaluated at the Moon's instantaneous position, and its imprint on the residuals is set by the projection map of an ephemeris held to a fixed dynamical time standard, not by a second screened charge. 2.2 Predicted Signal in LLR

The residual-channel estimand is extracted on the standard synodic basis: a fitted modulation of the Earth-Moon O–C range residuals as the Earth-Moon system orbits the Sun, of the form:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:range_perturbation} \delta r = 13 \eta_{\rm resid} \cos(D) \end{equation}

The predicted amplitude scales linearly: $|\delta r| = 13 |\eta_{\rm resid}|$ meters. For the order-of-magnitude estimate $\eta_{\rm resid} \sim 10^{-4}$, this gives $\sim$1.3 mm. The headline precision-weighted estimate ($\eta_{\rm resid} = -3.91 \times 10^{-4}$, Section 4.1) predicts an amplitude of $\sim$5.1 mm, while the leverage-excised robustness check ($\eta_{\rm resid} \approx -3.6 \times 10^{-4}$) predicts $\sim$4.7 mm and the naïve cosD-only OLS robustness check ($\eta_{\rm resid} \approx -3.18 \times 10^{-4}$) yields $\sim$4.13 mm. These amplitudes are small compared to the centimetre-level residual RMS, but with 26,207 observations over 35 years, statistical averaging provides sensitivity well below the per-observation noise floor.

2.3 Continuous Gradient Suppression and the Saturation Scale

TEP incorporates a Temporal Shear screening mechanism that suppresses scalar field effects in dense environments. The suppression transition is governed by the body's gravitational compactness ($\Phi/c^2$), which determines the degree of Temporal Shear suppression and thus the effective scalar coupling. Screening in TEP is tiered, not a single ambient-density switch. The asymptotic saturation scale $\rho_T \approx 20$ g/cm³ (Paper 6) marks where extended self-gravitating sources flatten Temporal Topology and suppress Temporal Shear in the Solar System. At galactic mean densities and in globular-cluster environments, gradient coherence can remain active despite local $\rho \ll \rho_T$, producing the larger observable response coefficients $\kappa$ reported in Papers 10 and 11. LLR therefore probes the strongly screened planetary channel: $\eta_{\rm resid}$ is the Solar System response coefficient, expected to be smaller in magnitude than galactic $\kappa$ while sharing the same screening ontology.

In the Earth-Moon orbit, the interplanetary ambient density ($\rho_{\rm amb} \sim 10^{-23}$ g/cm³) is far below $\rho_T$, but Earth and Moon remain differentially screened through their distinct compactness and interior shielding profiles. The TEP framework further predicts that this coupling is not static but environmentally modulated by the local scalar potential $\phi(r_\odot)$, which scales with heliocentric distance. Continuous TEP suppression near a saturation-scale transition implies that the effective Nordtvedt parameter $\eta_{\rm resid}$ varies with heliocentric distance $r_\odot$. When the Earth-Moon system moves through regions where the background scalar field approaches the suppression saturation scale, the effective coupling modulates as:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:orbital_modulation} \eta_{\rm resid}(r_\odot) = \eta_0 \left( 1 + m \cdot \frac{r_{\rm mean} - r_\odot}{r_{\rm mean} \cdot e_\oplus} \right) \end{equation}

where $\eta_0$ is the baseline Nordtvedt parameter at mean orbital distance $r_{\rm mean}$, $e_\oplus \approx 0.0167$ is Earth's orbital eccentricity, and $m$ is the modulation depth characterizing the nonlinear response. For $m \approx 1$ (consistent with the observed sign-flip), the orbital modulation generates diagnostic sidebands at frequencies $D \pm l'$ (where $l'$ is the orbital longitude), spectrally orthogonal to classical multi-body resonances. This provides a unique signature distinguishing TEP from standard PPN or systematic artifacts.

The TEP framework further predicts that the coupling depends not only on the scalar gradient magnitude (distance) but also on the rate at which the Earth-Moon system traverses the temporal topology. A body moving through a spatial scalar gradient $\nabla\phi$ at velocity $\mathbf{v}$ experiences an effective temporal shear rate $d\phi/dt \approx \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla\phi \approx v_r \, |\nabla\phi|$, where $v_r$ is the heliocentric radial velocity. For a Kepler orbit with small eccentricity, distance $r$ and radial velocity $v_r$ are approximately in quadrature ($90^\circ$ out of phase), making them statistically distinguishable predictors. The full dynamical modulation therefore takes the form:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:velocity_modulation} \eta_{\rm resid}(r_\odot, v_r) = \eta_0 \left( 1 + m_r \cdot \frac{r_{\rm mean} - r_\odot}{r_{\rm mean} \cdot e_\oplus} + m_v \cdot \frac{v_r}{\bar{v}_r} \right) \end{equation}

where $\bar{v}_r$ is the characteristic radial velocity scale ($\approx 0.5$ km/s for Earth's orbit) and $m_v$ is the velocity modulation depth. A joint fit to both $r_\odot$ and $v_r$ determines whether the temporal topology is purely static ($m_v = 0$) or dynamically responsive ($m_v \neq 0$). The heliocentric radial velocity analysis tests this prediction directly, using DE440 ephemeris velocities computed via Skyfield for every LLR observation epoch.

A further TEP-motivated probe uses a fixed celestial axis tied to the Planck 2018 dipole as an operational reference: $(l, b) = (264.02^\circ, 48.25^\circ)$ in galactic coordinates, equivalent to $(\alpha, \delta) = (168.14^\circ, -7.22^\circ)$ in J2000 equatorial coordinates, with amplitude $v_{\rm CMB} \approx 369$ km/s for the kinematic dipole. Some scalar-field embeddings single out a large-scale rest frame; the LLR regressions reported here do not, by themselves, establish Lorentz violation or uniqueness of that frame. They test whether residual-channel structure is consistent with anisotropic coupling defined relative to $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\rm CMB}$ and with other fixed axes used as controls (Section 4.25.2). Two operational predictions tied to $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\rm CMB}$ are:

First, an annual velocity projection: Earth's orbital velocity ($\sim 30$ km/s) projects onto the CMB dipole direction with a sinusoidally varying parallel component $v_\parallel(t) = \mathbf{v}_{\rm orb} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\rm CMB}$. The CMB dipole lies at ecliptic longitude $\approx 173^\circ$, offset by approximately $70^\circ$ from the perihelion longitude ($\approx 103^\circ$), making the annual velocity projection phase-shifted relative to the heliocentric distance modulation and therefore statistically distinguishable.

Second, a monthly orientation anisotropy: the Earth-Moon line sweeps across the celestial sphere with synodic period. If an effective gradient projection onto $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\rm CMB}$ is present in the same channel, the coupling should depend on the cosine of the angle $\theta$ between the Earth-Moon vector and that axis:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:cmb_anisotropy} \eta_{\rm resid} = \eta_0 \left( 1 + m_{\rm CMB} \cdot \cos\theta_{\rm EM-CMB} \right) \end{equation}

where $\cos\theta_{\rm EM-CMB} = \hat{\mathbf{r}}_{\rm EM} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\rm CMB}$. Because the Earth-Moon line rotates on a monthly timescale while the synodic signal operates on a 29.5-day period, the two modulations are at different frequencies and can be separated in a joint fit. The CMB dipole orientation analysis tests both predictions directly.

For the Earth-Moon system, the TEP potential produces a density-dependent effective mass $m_{\rm eff}(\rho)$ that grows with local density (see the TEP framework paper, Paper 0, §7), limiting the scalar field range inside massive objects. For a body in the strongly suppressed regime, this translates to a suppressed effective coupling $\alpha_{\rm eff} \ll \alpha_0$, where the degree of suppression scales with the body's surface gravitational potential (see the TEP framework paper, Paper 0, §7). Earth ($\Phi_{\oplus}/c^2 \approx 7 \times 10^{-10}$) experiences stronger Temporal Topology flattening than the Moon ($\Phi_{\rm Moon}/c^2 \approx 3 \times 10^{-11}$), giving Earth substantially stronger self-suppression and a smaller effective coupling to $\phi$. This large differential in gravitational compactness — and the resulting differential $\alpha_{\rm eff}$ — provides the physical basis for a Nordtvedt effect in TEP.
